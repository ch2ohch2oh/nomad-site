01

Two goals. Different routes.

Work temporarily

A work permit gives you temporary permission to work in Canada, with conditions and an expiry date.

Explore work permits

Settle permanently

Permanent residence (PR) lets you live and work in Canada on an ongoing basis, subject to permanent resident obligations.

Explore permanent residence

A visa or eTA is for travel. It does not by itself authorize work or guarantee entry.

You can explore permanent residence directly if you meet a program’s requirements. Working or studying in Canada first is not required for every route, and does not guarantee PR.

03

Work permits

Temporary permission to work, with conditions and an expiry date.

Employer-specific

Names the employer you can work for. The employer may need an LMIA or an exemption before you apply.

Open permit

Lets you work for most compliant employers. You must qualify for an eligible category; having no job offer is not enough.

Employer-specific work permit

Workers with a Canadian job offer

A permit tied to one named employer. Your employer may need a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) or an exemption, and must provide the required documents.

Read the work permit guide
Requirements & application steps for Employer-specific work permit

Where to begin

Ask your employer whether this job needs an LMIA or qualifies for an exemption, and which documents they will provide.

Requirements to check

  • A Canadian job offer and the documents required for the applicable permit category.
  • An LMIA or an applicable exemption, plus the general work permit requirements.

Application stages

  1. Confirm the permit category with your employer.
  2. Obtain the employer documents and apply using the relevant IRCC instructions.
Job offer
Yes — your employer must also provide the required permit documents.
What else matters
Canadian experience is not generally required; the job, employer documents and your qualifications matter.
Duration & status
Set by the approved permit and its conditions. Renewal requires a separate eligibility check.
Long-term route
A separate permanent residence application is required. A job or permit alone does not grant PR.

Francophone Mobility

French speakers working outside Quebec

An employer-specific route for French speakers with a job outside Quebec. No LMIA is needed; the employer still completes the offer and compliance steps.

Check Francophone Mobility requirements
Requirements & application steps for Francophone Mobility

Where to begin

Check the French-language and job requirements with your employer before they submit the offer.

Requirements to check

  • French speaking and listening at NCLC level 5 or above, plus general work permit eligibility.
  • An eligible job offer outside Quebec; certain primary agriculture jobs in TEER 4 and 5 are excluded.

Application stages

  1. Your employer submits the offer through the Employer Portal and pays the compliance fee.
  2. Use the offer number and evidence of French ability to apply for the work permit.
Job offer
Yes — an eligible job outside Quebec.
What else matters
French speaking and listening ability and job location matter; Canadian study is not required.
Duration & status
Temporary; check the approved permit for its expiry and employer conditions.
Long-term route
This is a work permit, not permanent residence. Compare PR programs separately.

International Experience Canada

Young adults from eligible countries

Work and travel based on citizenship, age and the categories available to you. Working Holiday is an open permit; Young Professionals and International Co-op are employer-specific.

2026 season: IRCC lists a profile deadline of 9 a.m. Eastern on 19 October 2026. Check the live season notice before acting.

Check the rules for your citizenship
Requirements & application steps for International Experience Canada

Where to begin

Select your citizenship on IRCC’s country eligibility page to see your age limit and available categories.

Requirements to check

  • Usually ages 18–35, or 18–30 for some countries; citizenship and category rules differ.
  • Working Holiday offers an open permit. Young Professionals and International Co-op use employer-specific permits and require a job or placement.

Application stages

  1. Check your country's categories, age limits, participation rules and available pools.
  2. Create a profile, wait for an invitation and submit the work permit application if invited.
Job offer
Working Holiday: no. Young Professionals and International Co-op: yes.
What else matters
Citizenship, age, category and past participation determine your options.
Duration & status
Depends on your country, category and participation history; check the country-specific rules.
Long-term route
Temporary work experience may be relevant to a separate PR program; IEC itself does not grant PR.

Post-graduation work permit

Graduates of eligible Canadian programs

An open permit for eligible graduates of qualifying Canadian study programs. A job offer is not required, but not every program qualifies.

Check graduate work permit eligibility
Requirements & application steps for Post-graduation work permit

Where to begin

Check your exact institution and program against the official eligibility rules, including language, field of study and deadlines.

Requirements to check

  • Meet institution, program, study status and application deadline requirements.
  • Language and field-of-study requirements can apply, depending on your program and study permit application date.

Application stages

  1. Check the exact program's PGWP eligibility before committing to study.
  2. After completing the program, gather the required evidence and apply within the applicable deadline, generally 180 days.
Job offer
No job offer required.
What else matters
Eligible Canadian study is required; your program and application dates matter.
Duration & status
Depends on the eligible program and passport validity; use IRCC's duration rules for your circumstances.
Long-term route
Studying and holding a PGWP do not guarantee PR. Eligible work experience may support a later application.

04

Permanent residence

Routes for settling in Canada. Experience, language, education, job offers and your intended destination can matter.

An invitation or nomination is a stage, not status. Each route still needs federal assessment and approval. A PR profile or invitation does not give you permission to work or extend your current status.

Express Entry

Eligible skilled workers and tradespeople

The application system for the Federal Skilled Worker Program, Canadian Experience Class and Federal Skilled Trades Program. Entering the pool is not an invitation to apply.

IRCC announced scoring changes on 16 October 2026, effective 3 November 2026. The scoring guide below separates the current grid from the upcoming rules.

Compare the three federal programs
Requirements & application steps for Express Entry

Where to begin

Compare the three federal programs on IRCC’s page and identify which experience and language requirements you meet.

Requirements to check

  • Federal Skilled Worker: qualifying skilled experience, language and education, plus its selection requirements.
  • Canadian Experience Class: qualifying skilled Canadian work experience and language ability.
  • Federal Skilled Trades: qualifying trade experience and language, plus a qualifying job offer or Canadian certificate of qualification.

Application stages

  1. Check that you qualify for at least one of the three programs; prepare language test results and required documents.
  2. Create a profile to enter the pool of eligible candidates. Your CRS score is used for ranking; selection also depends on the invitation round.
  3. If invited, submit your permanent residence application with evidence and fees. IRCC then assesses it and makes a decision.
Job offer
Not always — requirements differ across its three programs; trades applicants need a qualifying offer or Canadian trade certificate.
What else matters
Canadian Experience Class needs Canadian work experience. The other two programs can accept qualifying foreign experience. Language, education and occupation also matter.
Duration & status
This is a PR application system, not a temporary work permit.
Long-term route
Approval grants permanent residence. Eligibility, ranking and selection are separate stages.

How Express Entry scoring works

Rules in force on 17 October 2026. Changes start 3 November.

First, qualify for a program

Federal Skilled Worker applicants need 67 out of 100 on its selection grid, as well as the minimum requirements. Canadian Experience Class and Federal Skilled Trades have different eligibility rules. The 67-point threshold is not your CRS score. Federal Skilled Worker eligibility

Then, compete on your CRS score

The Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) ranks eligible profiles out of 1,200 points. Entering the pool does not guarantee an invitation.

There is no single passing CRS score

IRCC chooses a round type and invites its highest-ranking eligible candidates. Program-specific and category-based rounds can have different cut-offs. Category-based selection changes which candidates compete; it does not introduce a separate category-membership bonus. At the cut-off, the profile submission date and time can break a tie. French-language draws

French can help in two separate ways: access to French-language invitation rounds and additional CRS points.

1. Qualify for the French-language category

  1. Be eligible for at least one federal Express Entry program: Federal Skilled Worker, Canadian Experience Class or Federal Skilled Trades.
  2. Have French results at NCLC 7 or higher in all four abilities: speaking, listening, reading and writing.
  3. Meet the instructions for that round. IRCC ranks eligible French-category candidates by CRS and invites the highest-ranking candidates.

The category is based on language, rather than a particular occupation list. Your federal program’s work-experience and other requirements still apply. French-category eligibility

Use TEF Canada or TCF Canada and IRCC’s conversion tables to check each ability, rather than an overall average. Results must be less than two years old when you complete your profile and when you submit your PR application. Accepted tests and score conversions

2. Check the separate French CRS bonus

Where PNP adds 600 points

An accepted Express Entry-linked provincial nomination gives 600 additional CRS points. In the example above, with no other additional points, 429 + 600 = 1029. The 600-point group cap still applies. A non-Express Entry nomination uses a separate PR process and does not give an Express Entry profile this bonus. How nomination points work

Job offers: today’s rules and the November change

Through 2 November 2026: a job offer gives no CRS bonus points, although it can still matter for program eligibility. Current CRS factors

From 3 November 2026: IRCC introduces a Labour Market Integration factor. It compares Canadian work-experience points, including a possible high-wage occupation bonus, with qualifying high-wage job-offer points and uses the higher result. These two scores are not added together.

Qualifying high-wage job offers can receive 50, 70 or 100 points; qualifying Canadian high-wage experience can receive an extra 20, 40 or 70 on top of work-length points. Eligibility depends on the official occupation list, not your personal salary alone. This reorganizes the existing Canadian-work component; do not add the whole new factor to an old score.

IRCC says profile recalculation may take up to two weeks, with invitation rounds paused during that period. Read the effective dates and new rules

Provincial Nominee Program

People matching a province's priorities

Provinces and territories nominate candidates who meet local needs and intend to live there. Each stream sets its own criteria and intake limits.

Find provincial program links
Requirements & application steps for Provincial Nominee Program

Where to begin

Open IRCC’s provincial links, choose where you intend to live, and check that province’s categories and whether applications are open.

Requirements to check

  • Meet the chosen province or territory's stream requirements and intend to settle there.
  • For an Express Entry stream, also qualify for a federal Express Entry program. Quebec and Nunavut do not have PNPs.

Application stages

  1. Identify an eligible stream and check its current intake status.
  2. Obtain a nomination and follow the applicable Express Entry or non-Express Entry PR process.
Job offer
Some provincial categories require an offer; others use different work, study or occupation criteria.
What else matters
The province, occupation and chosen category matter. Some require a local connection; Canadian experience is not universal.
Duration & status
Nomination is a step toward PR, not a grant of PR or permission to work.
Long-term route
IRCC assesses the permanent residence application after nomination.

IRCC: PNP and provincial links

Workers and eligible Atlantic graduates

A route for eligible workers and Atlantic graduates settling in New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island, or Newfoundland and Labrador.

Requirements & application steps for Atlantic Immigration Program

Where to begin

Check the candidate requirements, then use the province’s official employer list to check whether an employer is designated.

Requirements to check

  • An eligible job offer from a designated Atlantic employer.
  • Meet the program's candidate requirements and provincial endorsement process.

Application stages

  1. Check candidate eligibility and the employer's designation.
  2. Follow the endorsement and PR application instructions. A separate temporary work permit option may be available.
Job offer

A PR pathway; a temporary permit while waiting has its own requirements.
Long-term route

Quebec skilled worker selection

Skilled workers intending to settle in Quebec

Quebec selects its own economic immigrants. Its Skilled Worker Selection Program (PSTQ) is separate from the Provincial Nominee Program.

Read Quebec’s selection rules
Requirements & application steps for Quebec skilled worker selection

Where to begin

Read Quebec’s PSTQ stream conditions and invitation rules before submitting an expression of interest.

Requirements to check

  • Meet the relevant PSTQ stream's conditions and selection criteria.
  • Receive an invitation before applying for permanent selection.

Application stages

  1. Submit an expression of interest and follow Quebec's invitation process.
  2. If selected and issued a Quebec Selection Certificate (CSQ), follow the federal PR application process.
Job offer
Depends on the Quebec selection category; an offer alone does not secure selection.
What else matters
You must intend to live in Quebec. French, occupation, experience and other criteria depend on the category.
Duration & status
Quebec selection and federal permanent residence are distinct stages.
Long-term route
A CSQ alone does not grant permanent resident status.

Terms explained
IRCC
Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada: the federal department that processes work permit and permanent residence applications.
LMIA
Labour Market Impact Assessment: a government assessment of the impact of hiring a foreign worker. The employer obtains it when required. An exemption removes this assessment, not the need to meet the work permit rules.
Stream
A category within an immigration program, with its own requirements.
Designated employer
An employer approved to participate in a particular immigration program. A job offer from any employer will not necessarily qualify.
NCLC
The Canadian language benchmark scale for French. Level 5 describes a required level of ability, not five years of study. Use the program’s rules to check accepted evidence.
TEER
A job classification based on training, education, experience and responsibilities. Your job duties determine the relevant occupation classification; your job title alone may not be enough.
CRS
Comprehensive Ranking System: the points system used to rank candidates in the Express Entry pool. Meeting a program’s minimum requirements does not guarantee an invitation.
Pool and invitation
A pool is a group of candidate profiles. An invitation lets you apply; it is not an approval or permission to work.
Nomination and endorsement
Support from a province under the applicable program. A nomination or endorsement is a stage in the process; the federal government still assesses the permanent residence application.
PSTQ and CSQ
PSTQ is Quebec’s Skilled Worker Selection Program. A CSQ is the Quebec Selection Certificate issued after selection; it is not permanent resident status.
Biometrics
Fingerprints and a photograph collected for an application when required.
PGWP
Post-graduation work permit: an open work permit for eligible graduates of qualifying Canadian study programs.
